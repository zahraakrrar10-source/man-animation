# VISUAL ART-DIRECTION BIBLE

Source: ~45 reference contact sheets/frames (vertical 9:16, 1080x1920 for the full-res frames). No code was inspected or changed.

## 0. How to read this document

Every claim carries a tag:

- **[OBS]** a direct visual observation from the frames.
- **[EST]** a measured estimate. Pixel values assume a 1080x1920 frame and are approximate.
- **[INT]** my interpretation of *why* it looks that way or how it was probably made.

**Important caveat.** I was never given an identified BridgeShort frame. Section 15 treats the flat-vector "headless chicken" set as the *possible* current-output proxy. That is an assumption the user has not confirmed. If that set is not BridgeShort, use only the generic checklist in 16.2.

## 2. Rules that appear in almost every Set A/B reference

1. **One shared character species.** Capsule/pill torso, oversized round white head, stick limbs, dot hands. [OBS]
2. **Face = a few flat marks.** Dot or oval eyes, short brow dashes, a one-line mouth. The expression carries the acting. [OBS]
3. **Identity lives in the hat and costume**, not the face: chef toque, tricorn with red-white cockade, bowler plus star badge, flat cap plus black eye-mask band, straw hat, bandana. [OBS]
4. **Heavy dark ink outline on characters and hero props**, thinner on the set, none or near-none on sky, water and far distance. [OBS]
5. **The world is genuinely 3D**: perspective, parallax, orbiting and tilting cameras, hard cast shadows, real depth. [OBS]
6. **Surfaces are painted, not photoreal**: wobbly plank lines, brick patterns, flat-colour blocks with a few tonal steps. [OBS]
7. **One dominant hue per scene** plus a complementary accent. The protagonist's costume colour separates from the set. [OBS]
8. **Light is directional and legible**: warm key, cool shadow, with glowing practical sources (candle, lantern, fire, window). [OBS]
9. **A sharp foreground element crosses the lower third**: deck rail, table edge, pot, banknotes. Mid-ground is the character, background is simplified. [OBS]
10. **Wide-angle, low or tilted camera** with strong leading lines to a vanishing point. [OBS]
11. **Captions sit at the same screen position**, about 70% down, centred, 1-3 words. [OBS][EST]
12. **Props are labelled and legible** (EVIDENCE crates, NATURE PRINTING sign, banknote design, PRICE OF SINS board, calendars). The set tells the story, not just frames it. [OBS]

## 3. Character design

### 3.1 Proportions
- Full-body shots: head about 1 unit tall, torso about 1.5, legs about 2 (roughly 4.5-5 "heads" tall). [EST]
- Head width is 1.2-1.5x torso width. Torso is a rounded capsule, about 0.7-0.8 of head width. [EST]
- Arms are a single curved line, about 1/20 head width, ending in a solid dot hand about 2x line thickness. No fingers except in rare gags (e.g. Franklin's wave). [OBS]
- Legs are two near-parallel thin lines, long relative to the body, with tiny or no feet. [OBS]
- Hand-drawn wobble: no perfectly straight limb. [OBS]

### 3.2 Face
- Heads are pure white and the brightest value in frame. [OBS]
- Eyes come in three modes: (a) deadpan, two black ovals under a flat half-lid line (chef); (b) wide, white sclera with a small pupil dot (shock); (c) closed arcs (joy). [OBS]
- Brows are short thick dashes that do most of the emotional work. [OBS]
- Mouth library: flat line, small "o", wavy worry line, open pink mouth, white teeth grid for strain. [OBS]
- Accents: pink blush ovals on some characters, blue teardrop sweat, black mask band for the crooks. [OBS]
- Face marks are crisp and flat even when the head is lit, so they read as decals on the head surface. [INT]

### 3.3 Costume language
- Costumes are flat saturated colours with a **single glossy specular streak** along one side of the torso (e.g. captain's blue coat, teal shopkeeper). [OBS]
- Hats carry a hard white highlight arc. [OBS]
- Costume silhouettes are instantly distinguishable at thumbnail size. [OBS]

### 3.4 Acting and poses
- Poses are simple and held: arms hang, point, or hold one prop. [OBS]
- Emotion is amplified with graphic add-ons rather than body deformation (sweat drops, sparkle eyes, shake lines, impact stars). [OBS]

## 4. Linework

- **Colour:** near-black, sometimes warm dark brown in warm scenes (tavern outlines read maroon-brown). [OBS]
- **Character head outline at close-up:** about 8-12 px on a ~470 px head, roughly 2% of head diameter. [EST]
- **Characters at distance:** head about 50-140 px with outline 2-5 px, so about 3.5-4% of head diameter. Outline gets *relatively* thicker as characters shrink; it does not scale linearly with size. [EST][INT: a minimum screen-space width, not a world-space width]
- **Hero props** (pot, ship's wheel, crates, candle): about 4-6 px, slightly thinner than characters. [EST]
- **Set pieces** (mast, rails, columns, windows): about 2-4 px or a thin tonal edge. [EST]
- **Sky, sea, fog, far skyline:** no outline. [OBS]
- Line quality: hand-drawn wobble and slight thickness variation, with occasional overshoot and open corners. [OBS]
- Back view of a character: simple blank grey head with the same outline (Treasury scene). [OBS]

**Rule:** line weight is a depth and importance hierarchy: heavy for what the viewer must read, light for what supports it, absent for atmosphere.

## 5. Materials and surfaces

- **Wood:** plank lines with irregular spacing and colour variation; warm orange base with darker grooves. [OBS]
- **Brick, cobblestone, tile:** simple repeating patterns with large, readable units (cobbles are chunky, not noisy). [OBS]
- **Fabric:** striped shirts, gingham, flags drawn as clean flat patterns; folds are minimal. [OBS]
- **Metal and glass:** pot rim has a yellow band and a pale gradient interior; highlights are single white shapes. [OBS]
- **Water:** blue with white caustic-style wavy streaks that move; splashes are outlined white cartoon shapes, not particles. [OBS]
- **Clouds:** flat-shaded cartoon clouds with purple-blue shading and an outline. [OBS]
- **Foliage and crowds:** repeated simple shapes (lollipop trees, repeated houses with chimney smoke puffs). [OBS]
- No visible photoreal specular, no ambient occlusion noise, no film grain in Set A/B. [OBS]

## 6. Environments: how much detail is actually there

**Detail budget per frame [INT]:** about 3 hero elements (tack-sharp, labelled, textured), about 5-8 supporting elements (simple shapes, one texture each), and everything else atmosphere (gradient, fog, silhouettes).

Examples:
- **Pirate deck:** planks, mast, rail, wheel, lantern, life ring; sea is a gradient plane with caustics; islands are fog-softened silhouettes. [OBS]
- **Counterfeit tavern:** two-tone red wood wall, lantern, table, chest, candle, banknotes. A sign provides the story context. [OBS]
- **Treasury:** columns with bunting, flag, steps, crates. The building is simple volumes with soft vertical shading and a thin line. [OBS]
- **Aerial town:** repeated house modules in 4-5 colours, chimney smoke, a spire as the landmark. [OBS]
- **Trial by fire:** stained-glass window, banners, torches, cobbles, stanchions, queue crowd. Crowd and far areas are blurred or simplified. [OBS]

**Modular set kit that recurs:** plank floor, cobble floor, brick wall, window with light shaft, banner pair, torch/candle/lantern, signage board, crate/chest, bunting, tent/stall, tablecloth. [OBS]

**Props carry period text and numbers** (year on calendars, price lists, labels). This makes small sets feel specific. [OBS]

## 7. Lighting, shadow and character integration

### 7.1 Light design
- **Golden hour/day:** warm orange key, cool blue-violet shadow side, pale haze at the horizon. [OBS]
- **Night:** deep navy gradient, one warm practical source (lantern, window, candle, fire) with a soft glow halo, and cool rim colour on everything else. [OBS]
- **Storm:** dark blue gradient sky, fog band at the horizon, desaturated far objects. [OBS]
- **Interior:** one dominant hue wash (e.g. tavern is almost monochrome red-orange) with a bright candle/lantern as the focal glow. [OBS]
- **Light shafts** through windows (church) and **vignette** on night/interior frames. [OBS]

### 7.2 Shadows
- Raking, hard-edged directional shadows on wood decks (mast and character shadows lie across planks). [OBS]
- Soft grey ellipse contact shadows under characters in outdoor wide shots (Treasury plaza). [OBS]
- Shadows are tinted (cool) rather than black. [OBS]

### 7.3 How characters are integrated
- **Head and torso take the scene colour:** heads go warm-yellow-white in firelight/tavern, lavender-blue at night; the head can be near blown-out against dark warm backgrounds. [OBS]
- **Torso has a gloss streak** and gradient that follows the key light; **limbs and face marks stay flat.** [OBS]
- **Characters cast and receive shadows** from the same light as the set. [OBS]
- **Hats and props** get real 3D specular. [OBS]
- [INT] This is partial 3D lighting on a stylised model: enough to sit in the scene, not enough to look like a CG figure.

## 9. Camera, depth, composition

### 9.1 Framing
- Subject centre of mass sits in the upper-middle (about 30-60% down) so the caption zone (about 65-75%) stays clear or overlaps only foreground. [EST]
- Close-ups: head 35-45% of frame width. Mid shots: two characters filling 60-80% width. Wide: characters 10-20% width, environment dominant. [EST]

### 9.2 Depth layers
1. **Foreground occluder**, crisp and large, often partly cropped (pot rim, rail, table edge, banknote sheet). [OBS]
2. **Mid-ground subject**, in focus. [OBS]
3. **Background**, simplified with fog or haze gradient (atmospheric perspective). [OBS]

### 9.3 Lens and angles
- Wide-angle with visible perspective stretch on foreground planes. [OBS]
- Frequent low angles, dutch tilts, top-down cranes (helm from above), and aerial reveals. [OBS]
- Leading lines: plank rows, diagonal rails, red-carpet corridor with repeating arches to a central vanishing point, road, rows of stalls. [OBS]
- Symmetry used for dread or scale (corridor, columns). [OBS]

### 9.4 Focus
- Mostly deep focus. Selective background blur appears in crowd/queue shots (trial by fire) and in dissolves. [OBS]

### 9.5 Camera motion (inferred from sequential frames)
- Push-ins and orbits around the subject. [INT]
- Pass-through moves: camera dollies through an arch/door into the next location. [OBS across sequential frames]

## 10. Phone readability

1. **One idea per frame.** Usually 1-3 characters and one hero prop. [OBS]
2. **Silhouette separation:** thick outline plus a contrasting costume hue against the background. [OBS]
3. **Value hierarchy:** white face brightest, hero prop second, background darker or hazier. [OBS]
4. **Big shapes:** chef toque, tricorn, wheel, pot all read at thumbnail size. [OBS]
5. **Caption lane** at about 70% height, short words. Backgrounds under the lane are calm (table edge, deck, floor). [OBS]
6. **Edge vignette** on dark scenes to hold attention centre. [OBS]
7. **Text on props is large** (EVIDENCE, NATURE PRINTING, ADMISSION) so it is legible at phone scale. [OBS]

## 11. Effects, diagrams and transitions

- **Cartoon FX:** speed/arc lines, shake lines, outlined splash, 4-point glints on gold, impact stars, chimney smoke circles, cone-shaped flames, radiating emotion lines. [OBS]
- **Particles** are bright dots/sparks around emissive objects (furnace, candle). [OBS]
- **Diegetic infographics:** calendars, ledgers, gauges, price boards, alarm panels, depth-gauge sleep chart. [OBS]
- **Blueprint cutaway** (Hungary): blue background, white stick figures, FIG. label, dashed paths. Used to explain a mechanism. [OBS]
- **Concept-as-place metaphors:** body as a house with alarm rooms, brain as ocean depth, stomach as a spa. [OBS]
- **Transitions:** cross-dissolve, match cut on a shape, push through an opening, white flash, whip. [OBS from sequence strips]

## 12. Reusable vs story-specific

**Reusable (build once):**
- Character rig and face-decal library (eyes, brows, mouths, sweat, blush)
- Outline system with a hierarchy (heavy/medium/light/none)
- Toon material and lighting presets (golden hour, night + practical, storm, interior wash)
- Sky/sea/cloud/fog kit
- Texture set: planks, brick, cobbles, tile, fabric stripes, gingham
- Camera presets (foreground-occluder close-up, low dolly two-shot, top-down crane, aerial reveal, corridor vanishing point)
- FX kit and diegetic-graphic templates (calendar, ledger, gauge, blueprint)
- Caption component

**Story-specific:**
- Costumes, hats and hero props
- Location set-pieces and signage text
- Per-story palette
- Concept metaphors

## 13. What changes between environments, and what doesn't

**Constant:** character proportions, outline hierarchy, lens feel, caption lane, face style, value hierarchy (white head brightest).

**Variable:** palette and dominant hue, time-of-day/light colour, set kit, fog density, vignette strength, camera height, prop density.

## 14. Why it looks 2D even though the world is 3D

[INT] The look is "3D for the camera, 2D for the surfaces":

1. Ink outlines with hand wobble flatten volume.
2. Flat colour blocks with only 2-3 tonal steps; no photoreal specular or ambient occlusion.
3. Face features are flat decals; limbs are single lines.
4. Textures are painted-stroke patterns, not scanned materials.
5. Shadows are hard-edged and tinted rather than soft and physically blurred.
6. Clouds, water splashes and smoke are outlined 2D shapes placed in a 3D scene.
7. Deep focus, so no optical-lens cue betrays a CG render.

The 3D shows up only in camera parallax, perspective convergence and cast shadows. Those three things are what sell "depth" without breaking the cartoon feel.

## 15. Set C (flat vector chicken farmer): analysis and differences from Set A

**Assumption:** Set C may be current BridgeShort-style output. It is unconfirmed.

### 15.1 Observations
- Flat 2D layers; backgrounds are simple vector shapes with soft gradients (sunset sky with a striped haze sun, barn, silo, trees). [OBS]
- Characters have **very heavy uniform pure-black outlines** (head outline about 10-14 px on a ~340 px head, about 3-4%) and thick black arms about 12-14 px wide. [EST]
- **Backgrounds and many props have no outline** (barn, stump, houses), while characters and chickens do, so characters read like stickers on a backdrop. [OBS]
- Chicken design is strong: scale-patterned feathers, teal/orange tail, thick outline. [OBS]
- Faces carry stipple freckles and soft diagonal light-streak overlays; blush ovals. [OBS]
- **Large dark dead zones**: near-black ground in the bottom 25-35% of outdoor shots and dark ceilings in the top 20-25% of interiors. [OBS][EST]
- Palette is narrow (crimson/purple/brown). The farmer's red plaid sits against a red barn and crimson sky, with weak hue separation. [OBS]
- Interiors use drawn pseudo-perspective (gingham tablecloth, plank floor) rather than real 3D. [OBS]
- Story props are good: dated calendars, signage, price list, receipt-style "MONTHLY TAKE". [OBS]
- Captions are yellow/red/white all-caps with black stroke at the same 70% lane. [OBS]
- Almost no camera motion implied by the stills; mostly front-on compositions. [OBS][INT]

### 15.2 Set A vs Set C

| Aspect | Set A/B | Set C |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Wide-angle, low/tilted, orbiting | Mostly flat front-on |
| Depth layers | Foreground occluder + subject + hazy BG | Subject on backdrop, little foreground |
| Outline | Hierarchy, warm-dark, wobbly | Uniform heavy black on characters only |
| Lighting | Directional key, cast shadows, tinted skin | Gradient/overlay mood, minimal cast shadow |
| Materials | Painted textures (wood, brick) | Flat fills |
| Colour | Complementary character-vs-set | Monochromatic, character blends in |
| Frame use | Full-bleed scenery | Black dead zones |
| Caption | Lowercase white, small word count | Uppercase yellow/red/white, loud |

## 16. Gap list vs "BridgeShort"

### 16.1 If Set C is BridgeShort (assumption)

1. **No real camera language.** Missing foreground occluders, low/tilted angles, orbits, vanishing-point compositions.
2. **Lighting is overlay, not scene light.** No directional key, no tinted cast shadows, no practical-light glow doing narrative work.
3. **Outline mismatch.** Heavy outlines on characters only; world has none. Needs a hierarchy.
4. **Flat materials.** Add painted plank/brick/cobble/gingham textures.
5. **Palette lacks separation.** Red character on red set; assign complementary hues and a clear accent.
6. **Dead black areas** waste phone screen. Fill with set depth or deliberate vignette, not empty black.
7. **Face/character shading is absent.** Add gloss streak, head tint, rim colour from the scene light.
8. **Transitions and FX** appear limited to cuts and calendar flips; add match-cut/push-through moves and the cartoon FX kit.
9. **Keep:** the distinctive chicken design, stipple/freckle texture, dated-prop storytelling, loud caption emphasis colours (as an option).

### 16.2 Generic checklist if BridgeShort is a different look (typical Three.js/Remotion shortfalls) [INT]
- Lighting too uniform (no key/fill separation, no cast shadows)
- Outlines uniform or missing (no hierarchy)
- Flat-shaded materials with no painted texture
- Camera static or linear (no occluder foreground, no lens distortion)
- Backgrounds either empty or too busy (no detail budget)
- Palette not designed per scene
- Characters not tinted by scene light
- No atmospheric fog/haze depth cue
- No dedicated FX kit

## 17. Acceptance checklist for any rendered frame

1. Is there exactly one focal subject, with the white face as the brightest value?
2. Is there a foreground element in the lower third that adds depth?
3. Is the outline hierarchy visible (character > hero prop > set > none for atmosphere)?
4. Does the protagonist's costume hue contrast with the set's dominant hue?
5. Is there one directional key light and at least one cast shadow?
6. Does the character's head/torso pick up the scene colour?
7. Is there a hazy, simplified background layer?
8. Is the caption lane (about 65-75% height) calm?
9. Is there at least one legible story prop with text?
10. Does no more than about 20% of the frame read as dead black?
11. Does it still read at thumbnail size?
